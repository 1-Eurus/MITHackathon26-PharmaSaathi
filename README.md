A healthcare facility running out of an essential medicine may initially look like an isolated inventory problem, but declining stock at several facilities can be an early signal of a wider supply disruption. Consumption patterns, replenishment delays, uneven inventories, and geographic constraints can cause shortages to spread before health authorities have enough time to respond. Without a shared view of demand and supply, available surplus may remain unused while another facility approaches stockout. Design a system that detects emerging medicine shortages and helps decision-makers understand how a local inventory problem could develop into a wider disruption. The system should reason about stock levels, consumption, replenishment, geographic availability, and possible redistribution or intervention opportunities. Teams may choose how shortages are forecast, how facilities and supplies are represented, how recommendations are prioritized, and how uncertainty is communicated. Simulated or public healthcare supply data may be used to demonstrate the approach.
